Decision 4

What glucose range defines TIR?

Which glucose range is used to calculate TIR?

Correct answer: B

According to the International Consensus on Time in Range, TIR is defined as time spent with glucose between 70 and 180 mg/dL. TBR is time spent with glucose <70 mg/dL, and TAR is time spent with glucose >180 mg/dL.

References

Battelino T, Danne T, Bergenstal RM, et al. Clinical targets for continuous glucose monitoring data interpretation: recommendations from the International Consensus on Time in Range. Diabetes Care. 2019;42:1593–1603.

Decision 5

What is an appropriate TIR target for Lisa?

Consider Lisa's age, comorbidities, and risk for hypoglycemia. What TIR target is appropriate for her?

Correct answer: A

A TIR of 70% is associated with an A1C of approximately 7%, and a TIR goal above 70% is recommended for many adults with diabetes. However, targets should be individualized based on age, comorbidities, and risk for hypoglycemia. Lisa is 71 years old, has longstanding diabetes and multiple comorbidities, including peripheral neuropathy and osteoporosis. Hypoglycemia avoidance is therefore especially important. A TIR goal above 50% with TBR below 1% is appropriate for Lisa.

References

American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee. 6. Glycemic targets: Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes—2022. Diabetes Care. 2022;45(Suppl. 1):S83–S96.

Battelino T, Danne T, Bergenstal RM, et al. Clinical targets for continuous glucose monitoring data interpretation: recommendations from the International Consensus on Time in Range. Diabetes Care. 2019;42:1593–1603.

Decision 6

What is an appropriate TBR target for Lisa?

Based on the same clinical considerations, what TBR (<70 mg/dL) target is appropriate for Lisa?

Correct answer: A

The recommended TBR goal for older and high-risk adults is below 1% for time with glucose under 70 mg/dL, meaning that hypoglycemia should be kept to an absolute minimum. For Lisa, minimizing TBR is especially important because falls and fractures could have serious consequences. As with all glycemic targets, the goal should be individualized.

References

Battelino T, Danne T, Bergenstal RM, et al. Clinical targets for continuous glucose monitoring data interpretation: recommendations from the International Consensus on Time in Range. Diabetes Care. 2019;42:1593–1603.

How could Lisa's insulin regimen better support her?

Lisa is sometimes forgetting insulin doses. Which options could help her keep track of doses or make insulin delivery easier to manage?

Select all that apply.

Correct answers: A and B

Cognitive impairment is common in older adults with longstanding diabetes and can make diabetes management more difficult. Missed insulin doses can contribute to hyperglycemia, while duplicate dosing can increase hypoglycemia risk. Connected insulin pens can record insulin doses and provide support that helps patients keep track of dosing. Insulin pumps can also support insulin delivery and may integrate with CGM, depending on the system.

Both connected insulin pens and insulin pumps require the person using them to be willing and able to use the technology safely, with appropriate training and ongoing DSMES. Lisa's A1C and CGM-based glycemic metrics are improving on insulin therapy, so replacing insulin with oral combination therapy is not appropriate. Continuing her current MDI regimen without additional dose-tracking support also does not address the problem she has described.

References

American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee. 7. Diabetes Technology: Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes—2022. Diabetes Care. 2022;45(Suppl. 1):S97–S112.

Srikanth V, Sinclair AJ, Hill-Briggs F, Moran C, Biessels GJ. Type 2 diabetes and cognitive dysfunction: towards effective management of both comorbidities. Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol. 2020;8:535–545.

Arrival

The moment is small, but it raises a bigger workflow question: how should a clinic reliably get diabetes technology data into the encounter?

There is more than one workable path.

Depending on the device and clinic workflow, data may be downloaded in the clinic, shared electronically before the visit, printed by the patient, or reviewed directly on the device.
